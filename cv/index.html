A public-facing snapshot of my engineering work across drones, climate sensing, fluid dynamics, hardware, and code.

Engineering Story

  • I build airborne measurement systems where mechanics, electronics, optics, and code all have to work at the same time. Most of my recent work sits around drones for climate and atmospheric science: small payloads, polarimetric imagery, high-rate data capture, CFD, controls, and field measurements that turn a flying platform into an instrument.
  • The common thread is practical research engineering. I like problems where a sensor has to survive the flight, a model has to explain the physics, and the software has to recover meaning from imperfect field data.

Research and Engineering Experience

  • 2025-26
    Drone-borne Cloud Droplet Retrieval
    University of Cambridge, Centre for Climate Repair context
    • Motivation and background
      • Marine cloud brightening research needs to know whether making cloud droplets smaller actually changes a cloud in the intended way during a field experiment.
      • Existing satellite and aircraft techniques are too coarse, expensive, or sparse to follow droplet-size changes across the small, evolving patches of cloud that matter.
    • Payload challenge
      • Developed a 500 g optical payload for a 7.5 inch drone, combining a polarisation camera, Raspberry Pi 4, SSD storage, separate flight and payload power, and a protective mechanical assembly.
      • Kept the sensing system independent from flight-critical power so the aircraft could stay safe while the camera and computer handled high-rate measurement.
    • Data challenge
      • Built the real-time capture path around the Raspberry Pi 4 to process and store up to 125 MB/s of polarimetric camera data without dropping the measurement stream.
      • Used the drone as a low-cost field platform while keeping the payload compact enough for repeated deployment.
    • Geometry and vision challenge
      • Wrote the Python video-processing pipeline for cloud masking, camera geometry, anti-solar-point tracking, angular profile extraction, and lookup-table fitting.
      • Used computer vision to turn unstable flight footage into calibrated cloudbow-region signals that could be compared with scattering-model predictions.
    • Most significant result
      • Retrieved cloud droplet size distributions at about 3 m by 3 m spatial resolution, roughly one to two orders of magnitude finer than current satellite and aircraft approaches.
      • Achieved fitting RMSE below 1% of peak signal magnitude and a measurement route that can cost less than 10% of current techniques.
